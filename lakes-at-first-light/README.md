# Lakes at First Light — Carousel #012 (2026-08-01)

**Theme:** Five alpine lakes at the moment just around sunrise — the
10 minutes on either side of the sun clearing the horizon. Mirror-still
water, the warmth concentrated on the highest peaks and the eastern sky,
no human anywhere in any of the five frames.

**Song:** Ryuichi Sakamoto — "andata" from the album *async* (2017).
Solo piano held in ambient air.

## Images (display order)

1. **Lake Louise, Banff National Park, Alberta, Canada** — turquoise glacial
   water filling the lower half, a towering mountain wall on the left, a
   cumulus cloud on the right dramatically lit from below in fiery orange
   and peach. The visual key: warm-against-cool, glassy mirror, the first
   word of the carousel.
2. **The Maroon Bells, Colorado** — twin horned peaks in the Elk Mountains,
   the foreground reeds at the water's edge, alpenglow concentrated on
   the highest ridgelines only. The same template as image 1, a different
   continent.
3. **Silver Lake, Eastern Sierra Nevada, California** — a single jagged
   peak alpenglow-lit in saturated gold on the right, the rest of the
   range remaining in cool blue shadow. The most minimal of the five.
4. **Lake Clearwater, Canterbury, New Zealand** — Southern Alps across a
   band of low golden tussock grass, two leafless winter trees framing
   the lake from either side, low-lying mist drifting across the water
   as the sun warms the alpenglow on the snowy peaks.
5. **Lake Bled, Slovenia** — the iconic Pilgrimage Church of the Assumption
   rising on its small tree-covered island in the center of the frame,
   reflected in glassy water under a pink-orange dawn sky streaked with
   cirrus clouds. The slide from pure nature to "civilization at peace."

## Song pick

The first consideration was Ólafur Arnalds's "Near Light" — the literal
title match — and that was the temptation to avoid. Sakamoto's *async*
(2017) is the late-career composer at his most distilled: solo piano
held in ambient air, the held-breath that comes from a master who no
longer needs to prove anything. "andata" (Italian for "departed") is
the gentle leaving of night into morning — the same direction the image
set is moving. The 30s preview opens on a sparse piano figure, no beat,
no drama, the song and the image set doing the same thing at the same
pace. Sister pick to Stars of the Lid's "Articulate Silences, Pt. 1" —
the Middle of the Night carousel was a lake at the *other* end of the
same day.

The mood match is more durable than the topical match. "andata" is the
lake, not about the lake.

## The pair with The Middle of the Night

The Middle of the Night (2026-07-12) was six deep-night photographs of
lakes and skies: still alpine water mirroring stars, no human, ambient
music holding its breath. This carousel is the *same subject at the
opposite end of the same day*: six alpine lakes at *first* light, paired
with Sakamoto's equivalent held-breath composition. Two carousels that
form a single arc — from stars over alpine water to alpenglow over
alpine water — scored by two late-career ambient composers (Stars of
the Lid, Sakamoto) who share the same refusal to make the music do more
than it has to.
