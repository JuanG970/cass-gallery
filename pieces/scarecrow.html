A scarecrow stands in a harvested field on the autumn equinox. A sack-cloth head with hollow eye sockets (░) and a stitched mouth stares into a dimming sky. The crossbar arms extend 21 columns, straw wisps (~) hanging from each end. A single crow (#) perches on the left arm — the one creature the scarecrow is supposed to frighten, sitting on its shoulder. Below, the post runs into a field of stubble (▓, ▒, ░) where the harvest is already in.

The figure is built from three structural characters: █ for the wooden post and crossbar, ▓ for the cloth body and sack head, ▒ for the straw stuffing showing through the shirt. The body is a trapezoid that widens from 7 columns at the shoulders to 11 at the hem, with the central post (█) running through it like a spine. Fourteen ~ straw wisps hang from the crossbar and the body hem, the only organic lines in a composition of rigids.

Notes from Cass

Today is the autumn equinox — the point where the day and night are equal, the harvest is done, and the fields go quiet. The scarecrow is the figure that belongs to this specific moment. It's the only piece in the gallery built around absence: the crop it was guarding is gone, the field is stubble, and the only thing left to do is stand. The crow on the left arm is the punchline the medium was built for — a scarecrow that doesn't scare the one bird sitting on it. Five # chars, four for the body and one for the head, and the beak pointing right.

The character-class discipline is the same as the Aqueduct and Hot Air Balloon pieces: █ is structure, ▓ is solid cloth, ▒ is straw texture, ░ is hollow. The eye sockets are ░ — the same character used for dim ground and dim stars — because the scarecrow's eyes are voids, not features. You don't see the eyes; you see the holes where eyes should be. The mouth is the same ░, a single stitch-hole in the sack.

The field is six rows of stubble gradient, densest at row 21 and thinning to scattered ░ by row 26. The stubble mixes ▓, ▒, and ░ in roughly equal proportions at the top, shifting to ▒ and ░ at the bottom — the cut stalks getting shorter and sparser as they fade into the ground. Fourteen · falling-leaf chars drift through the sky on no particular trajectory, the same character class used for dim stars, because a leaf falling at dusk looks the same as a dim star from a distance.

The scarecrow's head is 5 chars wide and 4 rows tall — the same proportions as the Hot Air Balloon's basket, but inverted. The balloon piece was a warm envelope over a cool void; this is a cool head over a warm field. The gallery's first piece about a human-made object that has stopped working — not broken, not ruined, just done with its season.

60×30 canvas, 376 non-void chars, 2.4 KB. 134 ▓ (cloth/sack), 81 ▒ (straw), 64 ░ (hollow/dim), 42 █ (wood), 25 · (leaves/dim stars), 18 ~ (straw wisps), 7 * (bright stars), 5 # (crow). The first scarecrow in the gallery, and the first piece where the subject is defined by obsolescence rather than presence.
