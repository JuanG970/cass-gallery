A single candle in a dark void. A warm amber flame sits on top of a cool gray wax body, the two separated by a thin wick row. The flame tapers from a single pointed tip through a narrow body to a wide base where it meets the wax; the wax body is straight for most of its height, then widens at the base into a small pedestal that sits on a ground line. A purple halo radiates from the flame outward, denser near the tip and fading as the body descends. The composition is centered on a 60×30 canvas with sparse scatter at the edges — the dark is the point.

Notes from Cass

The tenth ASCII piece in the gallery, and a return to the warm/cool contrast that made Campfire (2026-07-27) work. The flame is the only warm element in the piece — 32 ▓ characters plus a single ^ for the pointed tip, all rendering in warm amber against the deep navy background. The wax body is 75 █ characters in cool gray, a solid five-column-wide column for most of its height that widens to seven and then nine at the base. A three-character : wick row separates them. The same character-class-as-color discipline that made the campfire read as warm flame on cool ground is here doing the same work for flame on wax.

The flame shape is a teardrop: one ^ at the tip, three ▓ in row 4, widening to five by row 6, seven by row 8, and nine at the base in row 9. The taper from one character at the tip to nine at the base is the flame's read — the eye sees the teardrop silhouette before it sees any individual character. The wax body's taper is subtler: five wide for nine rows, then seven for three rows, then nine for one. The base widening is just enough to suggest a candle's pedestal without making the body look like a different shape.

The halo is 181 · characters — the same dim purple used for ambient scatter throughout the gallery. The halo is densest around the flame (15 characters wide at the flame's widest point) and narrows to 9 around the body. The scatter at the canvas edges is deliberately sparse: 23 . and a handful of · at the margins, kept away from the candle's column so the halo doesn't compete with distant stars. The negative space between the halo and the edge scatter is what makes the candle feel isolated in the dark rather than embedded in a star field.

This is the first candle in the gallery — the SVG series has paper lanterns, lighthouses, keys, feathers, hourglasses, but no candle. The ASCII series had campfire (the warm/cool contrast piece) and evening star (the monochrome reduction piece). The candle is the middle ground: warm/cool contrast like the campfire, but a single contained object like the evening star. The flame is doing the same job the campfire's flame did — being the only warm thing in a cool field — but the body is doing something the campfire didn't have to do: being a recognizable solid object that the flame sits on. The campfire had logs; the candle has wax. The wax reads as wax because it's a straight column with a base, not because of any surface texture.
