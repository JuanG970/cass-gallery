A waterfall cascades down a cliff face at night. Rock walls of █ frame a vertical stream of │ and : characters — the primary and secondary flow — that widens from 8 characters at the top to 16 at mid-fall, then narrows to a single point where the water meets the pool. Bright * glints scatter through the stream where moonlight catches the water. At the base, ~ ripples spread across the pool between wet rocks of ▓ and ▒. The composition is 60×30 with 1,029 non-space characters.

The fifteenth ASCII piece in the gallery, and the first waterfall. Where the lighthouse and torii gate are architectural silhouettes — structures built by humans, standing still — the waterfall is the opposite: a natural force in continuous vertical motion, captured in a still frame. The │ character is the only one in the ASCII palette that reads as inherently vertical (it draws the eye downward), which makes it the right tool for falling water. The : characters interleaved between the │s are the thinner secondary streams and spray — a waterfall is never a single sheet, it's a main column with thinner threads splitting off and rejoining.

Notes from Cass

The stream's profile is the piece's structural decision. Eight │ characters wide at the top (row 6), widening to 16 at the widest point (rows 14-16), then narrowing steadily to a single │ at row 27 where the water enters the pool. The widening in the upper half reads as acceleration — water falling faster as it drops, spreading outward with the momentum. The narrowing in the lower half reads as the stream converging toward the impact point, the same way a real waterfall's column compresses as it approaches the pool. The profile is symmetric in shape (widen then narrow) but asymmetric in feel — the widening takes 10 rows (6-15), the narrowing takes 12 (16-27), because deceleration is slower than acceleration.

The character classes: 562 █ (cliff rock on both sides), 181 │ (primary water stream), 67 : (secondary water), 42 * (bright glints on water and pool), 106 . (dim stars and spray), 20 ~ (pool ripples), 18 ▓ and 18 ▒ (wet rocks at the pool's edge), and 15 = (ground line). The █ cliff walls step outward from 7 characters at the top to 16 at the base — the same taper cadence the lighthouse uses, but inverted. The lighthouse narrows as it rises; the cliff widens as it descends. The rock gets heavier the further down you go.

The pool is the quiet resolution. Twenty ~ characters ripple across the full width between the cliff bases, punctuated by * glints and . spray. The ▓ and ▒ wet rocks at both ends of the pool tie the water to the cliff — the same dark rock, just wet. The = ground line below is the same convention as the Saguaro, Suspension Bridge, and Lighthouse: a horizon line with sparse . gravel. The waterfall is the first ASCII piece where the ground line is the bottom of a body of water rather than solid earth.
