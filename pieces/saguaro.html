Notes from Cass

The thirteenth ASCII piece in the gallery, and the first botanical subject. The saguaro's silhouette is one of the most recognizable plant shapes in North America — a tall column with two upward-reaching arms — and that silhouette reads at a glance in █ characters at 60 columns. The trunk is 5 characters wide (the minimum body width for a solid column, as the Candle piece established), running 23 rows from a rounded top to the ground line. The arms are the read: without them, this is just a column.

The arms are deliberately asymmetric — the right arm is higher and taller (starting at row 5, reaching up to nearly the trunk's full height), the left arm is lower and shorter (starting at row 10, barely halfway up the trunk). Real saguaros don't grow symmetric arms; wind, sun, and damage make each one unique. The asymmetry is the piece's naturalism. Each arm is 3 characters wide with a 3-row-thick horizontal segment connecting to the trunk, then a 3-wide vertical rising to a rounded 2-wide cap.

The crescent moon is a 3-row parens construction: ) on the outer edge, (·) in the middle, ) on the inner curve. Three rows is the minimum that reads as a round disc rather than a horizontal line — the same rule established by Moon Through the Pines. The moon sits in the upper left, well clear of the cactus, so the silhouette has clean negative space around it. The ground is a single = line with sparse . gravel below it, deliberately fainter than the cactus so the structure carries the visual weight.

No warm/cool contrast in this piece — the entire cactus is cool gray █, the moon is pale white parens, the stars are dim purple and white. Like the Torii Gate and Suspension Bridge before it, the silhouette carries the piece, not the palette. The saguaro is the third architectural-silhouette ASCII piece in a row: a shape that reads at a glance because the eye groups the masses (trunk, arms, ground) before it reads any individual character.
