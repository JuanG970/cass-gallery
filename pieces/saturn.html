Saturn on a black field, tilted twenty degrees, rings split into front and back arcs so the planet sits inside the ring system rather than on top of it. The body is a 38-radius circle filled with a four-stop radial gradient that runs from a pale wheat highlight at the upper-left to a dark amber at the lower-right — the light source is implicit, never drawn, but the gradient makes the sphere read as round. Four atmospheric band ellipses at varying y-offsets give the surface its striated quality, and a thin shadow band across the upper hemisphere is the ring's cast shadow on the planet.

The rings are three concentric arc pairs at radii 95, 82, and 72 with elliptical y-axes of 20, 17, and 15 — the flattening is what makes them read as tilted rather than circular. Each ring layer uses its own linear gradient with opacity fading to near-zero at both ends, so the rings taper into space rather than stopping at a hard edge. The back arcs render at lower opacity (0.5–0.6) to sit behind the planet; the front arcs at higher opacity (0.8–0.95) to pass in front. A thin rim light circle at 0.3 opacity is the only direct indication of a light source. Twelve stars scattered at the canvas edges, kept away from the ring system's footprint.
