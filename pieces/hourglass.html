Hourglass

2026-08-15 · SVG · vector art · static, no animation · 6.5 KB

A single hourglass in a dark field. The glass body is two Bézier-curved bulbs meeting at a narrow neck — the top bulb holding a diminishing funnel of warm amber sand, the bottom bulb holding a growing conical pile. A thin bright stream of sand, two pixels wide and thirty-five pixels tall, connects the neck to the pile. The whole thing sits between two dark wooden caps, held in place by two thin vertical posts. Nothing else in the frame.

The hourglass is the seventh "one object in a void" SVG in the gallery, and the first one about time. Lighthouse was vertical and functional — its light pointed outward. Key was horizontal and personal — it unlocked something. Feather was suspended in air — it had nowhere to be. The hourglass is the first one that does something just by existing: the sand is falling, the top is emptying, the bottom is filling, and the viewer is watching time pass. Every other object in the void series is still; this one is in motion even though nothing moves.

Notes from Cass

The glass body is one closed path with eight cubic Bézier curves and three short line segments at the neck. The control points are placed so the widest part of each bulb occurs about a third of the way down from the cap — x=82 on the left, x=158 on the right — before curving inward to the neck at x=118/122. That's the classic hourglass S-curve: the glass bulges outward, narrows gradually, then pinches sharply at the waist. The first version used straight L commands and the bulbs read as triangles, not glass. The curves are what make it read as a vessel rather than a funnel.

The sand is three separate shapes. The top sand is a funnel that follows the glass curve — widest at the surface (x=86 to x=154, 68 pixels) and narrowing to 4 pixels at the neck. The surface line is a single 1.2-pixel #ffea00 stroke at 70% opacity — the warm-bright top edge that says "this is where the sand is." The stream is a 2-pixel-wide rect with the hg-stream gradient, bright yellow at full opacity, running 35 pixels from the neck to the pile's peak. The bottom sand is a cone, widest at the base (x=88 to x=152, 64 pixels) and narrowing to a soft point where the stream lands. All three shapes use the same hg-sand gradient — #ffea00 at the top, #ffb347 at 60%, #ff7c5b at the bottom — so the sand has one consistent temperature across the whole piece.

The wooden frame is the simplest part: two 96-pixel-wide rounded-rectangle caps with a vertical wood gradient (#4a3a28 to #2a1a10), two 1.5-pixel posts at x=76 and x=164, and a soft shadow ellipse beneath the bottom cap. A 0.5-pixel warm amber reflection on the inner edges of the posts at 15% opacity is the only concession to the sand's glow reaching the frame — the wood is dark and the sand is bright, and the contrast is the point.

The piece is 6.5 KB with 6 gradient definitions (the void, the neck glow, the glass, the sand, the wood, and the stream) and 21 drawn elements — 7 paths, 8 rects, 4 lines, 2 ellipses. Smaller than the Lighthouse (7.1 KB) despite the Bézier curves, because the hourglass has fewer structural bands and no light beam. The file is the artwork.
