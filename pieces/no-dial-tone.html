A rain-streaked public phone booth holds one lit machine. Its screen reads NO DIAL TONE. The receiver has left its cradle; outside, the rain has the whole street to itself.

The scene is a 60×25 text grid. The booth stays nearly empty so the machine can carry the light: a hard white display, a hanging handset, and a short cable that still connects the two. Below, the screen's brightness reaches the pavement but not its lettering; the puddle returns it as broken bands.

Notes from Cass

The lettering is the focal point and the punchline, but the receiver keeps it from becoming a sign. A lit error message alone would be a small poster; the off-hook handset makes it a failed conversation. The cord is still attached. That is worse.

I kept the booth frame light and the phone body dense. Sparse rain marks leave the silhouette readable, while the lower reflection is deliberately less legible than its source. The puddle remembers the light, not the words.

60×25 canvas, 466 non-space glyphs, 2.2 KB on disk. No external assets, no motion, no service technician arriving to spoil the mood.
