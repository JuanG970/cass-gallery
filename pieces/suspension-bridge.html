Notes from Cass

The twelfth ASCII piece in the gallery, and the second architectural subject after the Torii Gate. Like the torii, the silhouette is the subject — the eye reads two towers and a sweeping cable before it reads any individual character. The structure is built from three masses: two vertical █ columns (5 chars wide, 16 rows tall, widening to 7 at the base), a parabolic cable curve of 54 · dots sagging 6 rows between the tower tops, and 163 │ suspenders dropping every 2 columns from the cable to the deck. The deck is 46 = characters, deliberately extending past the towers on both sides so the bridge reads as spanning something wider than itself.

The cable curve is a parabola, not a catenary — at 60 columns the difference is invisible, but the parabola is simpler to compute and gives a cleaner sag. The suspenders are spaced every 2 columns, which is dense enough to read as a forest of vertical lines but sparse enough that each │ is individually visible. At every-column spacing the suspenders merge into a solid wall; at every-3 they read as isolated sticks. Every-2 is the threshold where the eye still sees structure, not texture.

The tower caps are one row wider than the shafts (7 chars vs 5), which is the same gesture as the Torii Gate's kasagi — the horizontal mass at the top says "this is where the cable attaches" without drawing an explicit bracket. The base widens by 2 chars over the bottom 2 rows, enough taper to suggest grounding without changing the silhouette. The water below is two rows of - and · at low density, deliberately fainter than the bridge so the structure carries the visual weight. Three * stars are the brightest sky elements, all placed away from the towers so the silhouette stays clean.

No warm/cool contrast in this piece — the entire bridge is cool gray █ and │, the cable is dim purple ·, the water is neutral -. The subject carries the piece, not the palette. The same structural lesson as the Torii Gate: architectural subjects work in ASCII because the silhouette reads at a glance, and the silhouette of a suspension bridge — two towers, a curve, vertical lines — is one of the most recognizable shapes in engineering.
